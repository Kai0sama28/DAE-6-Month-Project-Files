1. Executive Summary

The Kali Linux host (192.168.64.3) is actively conducting adversary-in-the-middle attacks against the local network segment. Suricata detected ARP spoofing where MAC 02:de:ad:be:ef:50 claims ownership of the gateway IP 192.168.64.1, plus LLMNR and NBT-NS poisoning responses originating from 192.168.64.50. The host also issued WPAD lookups over both LLMNR and NBT-NS, consistent with Responder tool usage. Concurrently, the system's SSH hardening regressed (CIS benchmark failures for LogLevel, IgnoreRhosts, HostbasedAuthentication, PermitEmptyPasswords, PermitUserEnvironment, PAM) and a large volume of package installs/removals occurred, likely staging attack tooling. No successful credential theft or lateral movement has been observed in the 10-minute window. The activity appears to be an active attack phase, not a compromise of the Kali host itself. Severity is High due to active network poisoning targeting the gateway and local name resolution.

6. Attack Narrative

At 19:01:09 UTC the Kali host logged three lightdm GUI sessions. Two minutes later a burst of dpkg activity began (219 events in ~10 minutes), installing and removing packages consistent with tool staging. At 19:11:10 UTC Suricata captured an ARP reply from MAC 02:de:ad:be:ef:50 claiming to be the gateway 192.168.64.1 (rule 100059). Earlier in the window Suricata also saw Responder-style LLMNR and NBT-NS responses from 192.168.64.50 (rules 100050, 100052) and WPAD lookups from the Kali host itself over both protocols (rules 100051, 100053). Simultaneously, SCA checks recorded six SSH hardening controls flipping from pass to fail (rules 19011), reducing the host's CIS score below 50% (rule 19004). The combined evidence points to deliberate deployment of Responder or equivalent for credential harvesting via LLMNR/NBT-NS/ARP poisoning, with SSH weakening to facilitate persistent access.
