1. Executive Summary

Wazuh detected a verified SSH brute-force attack (rule 100002, level 12) targeting the local user 'labtester' on the Kali endpoint (192.168.64.3). The attack originated from the same host (localhost), not an external IP. Two distinct bursts of failed login attempts occurred over a 26-minute window: the first at 17:55:46–17:55:58 UTC (12 seconds, ~30 failures) and the second at 18:12:47–18:13:01 UTC (14 seconds, ~16 failures). No successful authentication for 'labtester' was observed. Earlier in the window, three legitimate GUI login sessions for user 'lightdm' were recorded. The activity is consistent with automated password guessing (T1110.001) but the local source raises the possibility of authorized testing, a misconfigured script, or insider threat.

6. Attack Narrative

At 17:46:23 UTC the Kali host recorded three lightdm GUI session opens/closes for user 'lightdm' (normal desktop activity). After a 9-minute pause, at 17:55:46 UTC a rapid series of SSH password failures for user 'labtester' began from 127.0.0.1 (logged as 192.168.64.3). Wazuh rules 5760 (sshd), 5503 (PAM), and 2502 (syslog) each captured the failures; rule 100002 fired three times during this first burst (firedtimes 5–7). Activity ceased until 18:12:47 UTC when a second burst of failures occurred, triggering rule 100002 four more times (firedtimes 1–4, the last being the trigger alert). Throughout the 26-minute window no successful login for 'labtester' was logged, no privilege changes occurred, and no file integrity events were recorded.
